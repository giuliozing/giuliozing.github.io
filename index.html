Publications

Agents in the Wild Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026 · Accepted

Securing Computer-Use Agents Against Branch Steering Attacks

G. Zingrillo, H. Foerster, I. Shumailov, Y. Zhao, R. Mullins

Abstract summary

Computer-use agents can be redirected by untrusted page content and tool responses even when execution follows an approved plan. This work studies branch steering as a data-flow integrity problem, introduces STEER-Bench to measure it, and presents COBRA: an architecture that constrains what each trusted branch may do across graphical and MCP interactions. The evaluation examines security, benign-task utility and the practical costs of enforcing these constraints.

EuCNC 2026 · Málaga, Spain

DDoS Adversarial Attacks Detection with Deep Neural Networks in P4 Programmable Switches

G. Zingrillo, L. Ismail, E. Paolini, F. Paolucci, F. Cugini, L. De Marinis

Abstract summary

Running neural intrusion detection directly in programmable switches offers low-latency protection, but adversarially modified traffic can undermine its predictions. This work adds an adversarial detector alongside the primary DDoS classifier, distils both models into lookup tables, and executes them together on an Intel Tofino switch. Experiments on Edge-IIoTset examine white-, grey- and black-box attacks and compare the hardware implementation with its software reference.

OECC/PSC 2025 · Sapporo, Japan

Assessing the Robustness of In-Switch Neural Networks Against Adversarial DDoS Attacks

G. Zingrillo, E. Paolini, N. Andriolli, F. Paolucci, F. Cugini, G. Contestabile, P. Castoldi, L. De Marinis

Abstract summary

This study examines how neural intrusion detectors behave under adversarial traffic once they are deployed in programmable network switches. It compares full-precision models with lookup-table implementations based on integer quantization, using both gradient-based and decision-based attacks. The experiments show that the quantization needed for switch deployment can also improve resilience to these perturbations.
